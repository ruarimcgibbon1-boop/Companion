# Bull Flags & Related Patterns — Research Compilation

Compiled 2026-07-31 from web research. Covers the bull flag family (chart patterns), adjacent bullish continuation patterns, and true bullish candlestick patterns, with the published statistics behind each.

---

## 1. The Bull Flag

### Definition
A bullish **continuation** pattern: a sharp rally (the **flagpole**) followed by a short, shallow, usually downward-sloping consolidation (the **flag**), which resolves with a breakout in the direction of the prior trend.

### Anatomy
| Component | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Flagpole | Unusually steep, several-day (or several-candle intraday) advance on strong volume |
| Flag | Small parallelogram/rectangle tilted *against* the trend (down or sideways drift) |
| Duration | Under 3 weeks on dailies — longer and it's a rectangle/channel, not a flag |
| Volume | Declines through the flag (74–77% of flags per Bulkowski), then spikes on breakout — ideally 50–100% above average |

### Trading the pattern
- **Entry:** break above the flag's upper trendline or the flag high. Intraday version: the first candle to make a new high after 2–3 pullback candles.
- **Stop:** below the low of the flag/pullback.
- **Target (measured move):** project the flagpole height upward from the breakout point. Entry $45 + $10 pole → $55 target. Bulkowski's more conservative version: multiply pole height by the historical hit rate (~46%) before projecting.
- **Confirmation filters that matter:** breakout volume spike; tight (not loose/jagged) flag; broad market not selling off.

### Bulkowski's flag statistics (thepatternsite.com, 1,000+ samples)
- Upward breakouts: **60%** of flags
- Break-even failure rate: **44%** (up) / 45% (down) — flags are *not* a high-probability pattern in isolation
- Average move after breakout: **+9%** (up) / −8% (down), measured to the short-term swing, not ultimate high
- Price target met: only **46%** of the time
- Best performers: **tight flags**, tilted downward after an up move, occurring near yearly lows
- "Half-staff" behavior (flag marks the trend midpoint) is real but unreliable as a precision tool

### Key insight
The ordinary bull flag is roughly a coin flip on its own. Its edge comes entirely from **context and filters**: pole quality, flag tightness, volume signature, market regime.

## 2. High & Tight Flag (HTF) — the elite variant

Bulkowski's standout pattern, and the one worth special treatment in any scanner.

- **Definition:** price roughly **doubles (≥90% rise) in 2 months or less**, then pauses in a tight consolidation. The consolidation often doesn't look like a textbook flag — it's just a pause.
- **Confirmation:** only valid when price **closes above the highest peak in the pattern** (usually the flagpole top). Don't buy trendline touches — those fail frequently.
- **Statistics (1,028 trades / 307 patterns):**
  - **85%** chance of a meaningful continuation in a bull market
  - Average post-breakout gain: **+39%** (some studies cite 69% average rise; none of 307 patterns failed to rise at least 5%)
  - Break-even failure rate: only **15%**
  - 82% meet the (half-height) price target; throwbacks occur 67% of the time — expect a retest
- **What improves it:** tight pattern, down-sloping top trendline, receding volume in the flag, ~45° flagpole slope (near-vertical poles actually do *worse*)

## 3. Pennant — the weak sibling

Same idea as a flag but the consolidation is a small **symmetrical triangle** (converging trendlines) instead of a parallel channel.

- Duration ≤ 3 weeks; volume falls through the pattern **86%** of the time
- Upward breakouts: 57%
- **Break-even failure rate: 54%** — worse than a coin flip; Bulkowski's data (1,600+ trades) makes pennants one of the worst-performing patterns
- Average move: only +7% / −6%; target met just 35% (up) / 32% (down)
- Momentum decays across the pattern: pre-pennant move averages +19% in 11 days, post-pennant only +14% in 10 days
- Practical takeaway: **treat a bull pennant as a lower-confidence bull flag**, and demand stronger volume confirmation

## 4. Failed bull flags — what breakdown looks like

Knowing the failure signature is as valuable as the pattern itself:

1. **Pullback depth:** once the flag retraces **more than ~50% of the pole**, demand is weaker than the pole implied. Beyond **~62% retracement**, treat the pattern as broken even if the shape still "looks like" a flag.
2. **The classic fakeout:** price pokes above the upper trendline, triggers breakout buyers, then closes back inside the flag — typically an ugly upper-wick candle on unimpressive volume.
3. **Low-volume breakout:** a breakout without a volume expansion is suspect; the good ones show a clear spike at the moment the first candle makes a new high.
4. **Market regime:** flag breakouts fail disproportionately when the broad market is red/selling off.
5. **Loose, jagged flags** (overlapping candles, meandering) underperform tight ones across all of Bulkowski's samples.

## 5. Adjacent bullish continuation patterns

### Ascending triangle
Flat resistance repeatedly tested while lows step higher — buyers absorbing supply at a fixed price. Breakout through the flat top continues the uptrend. Target: height of the triangle projected from the breakout.

### Falling wedge
Lower highs and lower lows inside *converging, down-sloping* trendlines; momentum of the decline shrinks until price breaks out upward. Works both as continuation (in an uptrend) and reversal (at the end of a downtrend). Confirm with volume on the break.

### Cup and handle
U-shaped correction (the cup: 20–50% deep, 7–65 weeks) followed by a smaller handle pullback (5–15%, 1–4 weeks), then a breakout above the rim. O'Neil-school studies claim ~80–85% of correctly identified formations break out successfully — but "correctly identified" is doing heavy lifting there. Essentially a bull flag on a larger timescale: big advance → proportionally small consolidation → continuation.

### Rectangle / flat base
Sideways box after an advance. A flag that lasts >3 weeks becomes one of these. Flags/HTFs forming **above a flat base** perform notably better (Bulkowski).

## 6. Bullish candlestick patterns (single/multi-candle)

Bull flags are chart patterns (many candles); these are the true candlestick patterns that often *compose* the flag's turning point — e.g., a hammer at the flag low.

| Pattern | Structure | Win rate (confirmed / unconfirmed) |
|---|---|---|
| Three white soldiers | 3 consecutive strong closes, each opening within prior body | ~**70%** — highest, but rare and often late |
| Morning star | Down candle → small indecision candle → strong up candle | ~65% confirmed; ~70–75% at key support levels |
| Bullish engulfing | Up candle's body fully engulfs prior down candle's body | ~63% confirmed / 47% raw |
| Hammer | Small body, long lower wick, at a low | ~60% confirmed / **41% standalone** |

### The universal caveat
Every study converges on the same conclusion: **candlestick patterns without context are sub-50% signals.** The three filters that flip them positive:
1. Location — at a key support level or the bottom of a defined move (a hammer at support ≈ 7/10; the same hammer mid-range ≈ 4/10)
2. Volume — above-average on the signal candle
3. Confirmation — a follow-through candle in the signal direction before entry

## 7. Intraday / momentum application (day-trading school)

The intraday bull flag is the canonical "**first pullback**" setup in momentum trading (Warrior Trading, Bulls on Wall Street, et al.):

- Stock squeezes up on an opening drive with real volume → tall green candles form the pole
- Wait for **2–3 red pullback candles on declining volume** — this is the flag
- **Entry:** first green candle to make a new high; volume should spike right there
- **Stop:** low of the pullback
- The *first* pullback after a fresh momentum move is the highest-quality one; each subsequent flag on the same move carries less fuel (mirrors Bulkowski's finding that post-pattern moves are weaker than pre-pattern moves)
- Same failure rules apply: >50% pole retracement, low-volume break, weak market tape

## 8. Synthesis — a quality hierarchy

Ranked by published edge, best first:

1. **High & tight flag** (≥90% pole in ≤2 months, tight, buy the close above pattern high) — 85% success, +39% avg
2. **Cup and handle** (properly formed, rim breakout on volume) — ~80% claimed
3. **Tight bull flag** with declining flag volume + breakout volume spike + healthy market — meaningfully better than baseline
4. **Ordinary bull flag**, no filters — ~55% baseline, near coin flip
5. **Bull pennant** — 54% break-even *failure* rate; demand extra confirmation or skip

Scanner-relevant scoring signals distilled from all sources:
- Pole steepness & size (bigger/steeper pole up to ~45° slope = better; parabolic verticals worse)
- Flag tightness (low range, non-overlapping structure)
- Flag volume contraction (present in 74–86% of valid patterns)
- Retracement depth vs. pole (<38% great, <50% acceptable, >62% invalid)
- Breakout volume expansion (50–100%+ above average)
- Market regime filter (index green/red)
- Position in larger structure (above flat base = better; late in an extended move = worse)
